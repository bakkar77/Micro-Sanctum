Our Project

Where Science Meets Soil

A Grade 11 Capstone project tackling microclimate instability and food waste at our school campus.

The Problem We Solved

Egypt's 6th of October City faces extreme microclimate swings — scorching midday heat above 32°C and cool nights dipping below 17°C. Traditional growing methods fail under these conditions.

We built MicroSanctum: a 40×62×55 cm automated greenhouse controlled by an ESP32 microcontroller, using DHT11 and BH1750 sensors to monitor temperature, humidity, and light — and actuators to correct them in real time.

Radish microgreens thrive inside while the Egyptian desert climate rages outside. Nothing is wasted: eggshells from our school restaurant are upcycled into fertilizer, seedling pots, and compost mixes.

🌡 Climate Automation

Exhaust fan cools when temp exceeds 25°C. Incandescent lamp heats when it drops below 20°C. Humidifier activates when air gets too dry.

💡 Smart Lighting

BH1750 light sensor manages the day/night cycle. Full-spectrum LEDs ensure microgreens receive optimal Lux during growing hours.

♻ Zero-Waste Ethos

Every eggshell from the school restaurant is collected, cleaned, and transformed into natural fertilizer or seedling pots — nothing goes to landfill.

🌱 Bottom Watering

Plants are watered from below to prevent mold, conserve water, and ensure even root absorption — a technique used by professional microgreen growers.
